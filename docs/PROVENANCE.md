## 2. NPC sprites — the md5 verdict

Rene's "NPC photos" = NPC character sprites (there are no portrait photos anywhere in the game).
Verified by direct md5 + pixel comparison against `characters/game-sheets/` and
`characters/replacement/img/characters/`:

- **Named cast (15 `$` sheets in this copy): DO NOT match our redesigns.** Different pixels,
  much smaller files (6–8 KB vs 30–38 KB) — these are Rene's earlier custom versions (class B).
- **NPC stock slots (People1-4, Actor1-3, SF_Actor2): DO NOT match our redrawn sheets.** Per the
  characters lane's own report, its VM-copy install was blocked by a write-guard window and only
  staged; the sheets here are pristine stock (class C). The lane's staged `stock-backup/` copies
  in the workspace are 0-byte placeholders from those blocked pulls — do not mistake them for
  evidence either way; the md5s above are the evidence.
- Our redraws (A) are verified present in `characters/game-sheets/` (15 named + 10 `$NPC_*`
  singles) and `characters/replacement/img/characters/` (8 sheets), and the playtest lane
  installed them on the Razer (504/504 battler + cast checks passed there).
- **Ship rule:** build the repo's `img/characters/` from the `characters/` deliverables, never
  from this copy's folder.

## 5. Licensing reality + replacement plan

**The reality, plainly.** RTP audio (and art) is © Kadokawa. The RTP license lets a licensed
RPG Maker owner use the assets *inside games made with RPG Maker*; it does **not** grant any
right to redistribute the asset files themselves — so the open-source repo cannot contain them,
period. Downloading a copy of the soundtrack and "building on it" creates no rights: an edit of
a recording you don't own is still their recording. Shipping the *packaged game* on Steam via
MZ's own deployment is the standard licensed route, but it keeps the game chained to the MZ/RTP
terms and collapses entirely for console ports or any non-MZ packaging — and this project's
whole design (open source + future console) needs music it owns outright. (Engineering rule, not
legal advice: **ship zero RTP bytes.**)

**Replacement plan — by slot, one primary route:**

**Primary route for v1: local procedural composition.** A small Python composition/synthesis
pipeline (chiptune-style square/triangle/noise voices + simple arrangement code) renders each
slot to loopable OGG with loop metadata. Rights are unambiguous (our code, our notes), cost is
$0, it runs offline on the Razer/VM, loops are sample-exact, and the aesthetic fits the pixel
art. **Suno is the optional later upgrade for the title theme only** — richer sound, but output
rights depend on Rene's plan tier at generation time and it adds another account dependency for
a 21-track problem it doesn't need to solve. Commissioning is unnecessary at this scale.

| Priority | Slots | Count | Route |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 — core loop | Title theme; Town A/B; City; Route A/B; Wild Battle; Gym/Leader Battle; Cave/Dungeon; Story theme (lab/intro); Tower theme; Interior (center/mart) | 12 BGM | Procedural composition |
| P1 — stingers | Victory, Defeat, Game Over, Fanfare (badge), Inn, Item-get | 6 ME | Procedural (short motifs) |
| P2 — ambience | City, Cave drips, Sea, Waterfall, Waves, Wind | 6 BGS | Procedural (filtered-noise soundscapes) |
| P2 — effects | The ~48 used SE (UI, battle hits, capture, doors, heals…) | ~48 SE | Procedural one-shot synth; CC0 packs (e.g. Kenney) acceptable for foley-style sounds |
| P3 — dormant | Ship1/2/3 vehicle themes | 3 BGM | Only if vehicles ever ship |
| Optional | Title theme "deluxe" version | 1 | Suno, later, if Rene wants a richer title track |

The gyms currently lean on Theme/Dungeon/Battle map music (7 different tracks across 8 gyms);
the P1 set covers them with Gym Battle + per-map field themes. Track count to compose for a
fully original-sounding v1: **12 BGM + 6 ME + 6 BGS + ~48 SE.**
