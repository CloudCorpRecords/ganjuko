# Ganjuko Balance Audit V2 — Chart v1.1 + Engine Move Pools

Generated by `work/sim/simulate2.mjs` (seed 20261101, same Lv50 protocol as v1).
Raw data: `work/sim/results-v2.json`. The v1 simulator and `results.json` are untouched.

## Method in one paragraph

Three modes over one continuous seeded RNG stream. **A** = chart v1 + raw
learnsets (the original sim). **B** = chart v1.1 + raw learnsets (isolates the
chart fix). **C** = chart v1.1 + engine Section 16.5 best-4 pools — the real
game — with `GanjukoEngine.augmentSkills` replicated line-for-line at Lv50
(slot plan `[statusPick, coverage, stabSecond, stabStrongest]`, `_s16_skillStrength`
scoring, tier gates, coverage table). Matrix samples are drawn once under mode
A and reused for B and C (paired comparison). Battle model is otherwise
identical to v1: greedy expected-value move selection, STAB 1.5, crit 1.5 @
1/16, roll 0.85–1.00, heals capped at 3 uses, status utility 30, no MP.

**Reproduction gate: PASS, exact.** Mode A reproduces the original
`results.json` bit-for-bit: identical matrix samples, identical 21,000
round-robin battles, max diff 0.0000 on every type overall and every species
win rate, identical top/bottom 20. Everything below is therefore comparable
to the v1 audit.

## 2. Mode B — what the v1.1 chart change did on its own

- The targeted cell moved exactly as designed: **Water attacking Nature went
  0.00 → 0.30** (0/20 → 6/20 matrix wins). Water overall 32.2% → 36.1%.
- Nothing else moved materially; the other deltas (Earth 61.1 → 54.4, Nature
  60.0 → 62.2, Astral 61.1 → 57.8) are stream noise, not chart effects — the
  chart change touches only Water's offense vs Nature.
- Verdict on v1.1: **correct and sufficient as a chart change, but not
  sufficient alone.** On raw learnsets Water (36.1%) and Normal (24.4%) remain
  broken. The pools, not the chart, are the main remedy — which is what mode C
  shows. No further chart change is indicated by mode B.

## 3. Mode C — the real game (v1.1 + Section 16.5 pools)

### The pools themselves

At Lv50 the slot plan is a pure function of type: the four fixed picks always
fill the pool before the learned-flavour pick or the backfill can ever be
reached (every type's `statusPick` is non-zero at Lv50), so there are exactly
**9 distinct pools** — one per type — regardless of species. Every pool fields
exactly 3 damaging moves + 1 utility/heal slot, and the engine's guarantees
verify clean across all 504 species: **0 species with < 3 damaging moves, 0
without a non-own-type coverage move, 0 pools not of size 4.** The 24
two-damaging-move final forms and the 20 duplicate-Tackle learnsets from the
v1 audit are fully absorbed.

| Fire | Rebirth Flame / Rock Throw (Earth) / Dragon Wrath / Sacred Fire | heal 80% |

| Volt | Brace / Wing Slash (Wind) / Thunder Blast / Lightning Storm | status |
| Earth | Obsidian Bastion / Spark (Volt) / Earthquake / Continental Rift | status |
| Wind | Brace / Dark Pulse (Umbra) / Tempest Dive / Hurricane | status |
| Umbra | Brace / Psybeam (Astral) / Eclipse Slash / Oblivion Descend | status |
| Astral | Brace / Night Slash (Umbra) / Divine Judgement / Genesis Radiance | status |

Why pools moved types the way they did — best-hit damage of pool vs raw
learnset against a neutral dummy (def/mdf 344), at each type sample's own
stats: Normal **1.72×**, Umbra 1.52×, Fire 1.37×, Water 1.32×, Astral 1.28×,
Earth 1.17×, Volt 1.00×, Wind 1.00×, Nature **0.92×**. Pools are a
redistribution: they hand the weakest raw learnsets (Normal above all) the
strongest moves in the game, and mildly *nerf* Nature, whose raw templates
were above the pool standard. Every type already fields the strongest moves
of its element that exist in Skills.json — the pools are at their data
ceiling (e.g. Mega Punch *is* the strongest Normal damaging move in the
game; there is nothing stronger to give Normal).

Design observation: raw data had 47 distinct learnsets; mode C has 9 distinct
pools. Within a type, species identity in battle now comes only from stats.
Given the 4-move cap and how templated the raw learnsets were, the practical
variety loss is smaller than the numbers suggest — but it is real, and it is
the price of the guarantees.

## 4. The Water matrix investigation (why 25.6% is not a balance fact)

Mode C's Water matrix overall (25.6%) contradicts its RR mean (49.0%). Four
independent checks say the matrix number is the artifact:

1. **Segment spread.** Re-running mode C's exact configuration (same sample,
   same chart, same pools) over 8 consecutive RNG stream segments: Water's
   matrix overall averages **37.4% with SD 4.5 pts (range 29.4–43.3)**. The
   published 25.6% is an unlucky segment, below the observed range. The
   matrix estimator's real noise (member draws with replacement from a
   heterogeneous 12-species sample) is several times the ±3.7-pt binomial
   figure the v1 audit quoted. Segment means for all types are in
   `results-v2.json`-adjacent analysis: Normal 36.8 ± 4.6, Fire 53.6 ± 2.8,
   Nature 42.8 ± 3.4, Volt 57.6 ± 1.5, Earth 61.0 ± 2.9, Wind 51.7 ± 3.1,
   Umbra 61.5 ± 2.3, Astral 57.5 ± 1.5.
2. **The sample is the weakest cohort in the game.** Water's 12 sampled
   final forms have the lowest mean BST of any type sample (2,822, from the
   v1 audit) and the lowest mean AGI (330 vs 347–370 for every other type).
   Three of the twelve are weak finals outright (#20 Crushcean RR 10.8%,
   BST 2,290, AGI 217; #14 Oceanlord 18.6%; #21 Serpente 23.6%), while the
   sample's strong members reach 89.5% (#282 Abysswingark). In a meta where
   pool cannons one-shot, a slow, light sample loses before it fires.
3. **It is not the moves.** Against a neutral dummy, Water's pool best-hit
   is 1.32× its raw learnset's — the pool *raised* Water's damage. Its two
   cannons (Absolute Zero, Abyssal Cataclysm) are in the same one-shot class
   as every other type's.
4. **Lever tests (stream-matched replays of A+B→C; harness validated by an
   absurdity probe — reducing Water's pool to Brace-only craters it to
   5.6%/6.0%, so mutated pools demonstrably reach battles):**
   - Swapping Water's finishers to Hydro Cannon/Maelstrom (the raw
     template's ×1.0-coefficient cannons): **zero flipped outcomes.** The
     swap changes expected hits-to-kill in only 6 of 1,284 sample pairings —
     both cannon pairs are deep in overkill territory, so the choice of
     finisher is outcome-irrelevant. This lever is dead.
   - Giving Water a heal slot (`statusOrHeal = [16]` Synthesis): **zero
     effect.** Under greedy expected-value selection, a heal (max score
     ~490) never out-scores a 1,400–3,400 cannon, and Water's damaging set
     is never all-immune against any type, so the slot is never used. This
     lever is dead in the sim model (and nearly so in the real engine,
     whose AI also scores heals below ~45% HP only).
   - Applying the engine's Stadium stat normalization to *all* battles:
     Water's matrix cell recovers to 46.1%, but Normal (31.1%), Nature
     (30.0%), Earth (62.8%) and Umbra (60.6%) break instead, and the
     final/unevolved RR means invert (47.8% / 51.6%). A reshuffle, not a
     fix — normalization must stay Stadium-scoped, as shipped.

## 5. Which numbers to trust

- **Round-robin means are the balance metric.** 21k+ battles over all 504
  species; per-type standard error ≈ 1 pt. Mode C: every type in band.
- **The type matrix is a final-form snapshot with fat error bars** (segment
  SD 1.5–4.6 pts, and single segments can land outside the observed range,
  as Water's did). Use its 8-segment means (§4) for final-tier statements,
  never a single run. On segment means, the final tier still tilts:
  Earth (61.0%) and Umbra (61.5%) over; Normal (36.8%) and Water (37.4%)
  under; the rest in or at the band edge.

## 6. Recommendations (no changes made — this lane only measures)

1. **Ship chart v1.1 + Section 16.5 as-is.** Dex-wide, the real configuration
   is balanced: all nine types in the 42–58% band on the metric that covers
   the whole game. The two v1 failures (Normal, Water) are fixed where the
   game is actually played.
2. **Do not chase the mode C matrix residuals with chart or pool edits.**
   Both candidate pool levers were tested to zero effect, and the chart was
   proven (mode B) to move only its targeted cell. The residual final-tier
   tilts track sample composition and BST/AGI distribution in Classes.json —
   editor-owned data, outside the engine's remit — and the one engine-side
   absorber that exists (Stadium normalization) is already shipped and
   correctly scoped to Stadium.
3. **Watch items for the next audit:** Nature's thin low-edge margin
   (RR 43.9%); Volt's high-edge matrix readings; Earth/Umbra final-form
   cohorts if Stadium results mirror the matrix tilt; #165 Terratailius as
   the last perfect-record species.
4. **Process fix for future audits:** report matrix numbers as multi-segment
   means. A single-seed matrix run can be off by 5–12 pts on heterogeneous
   samples (Water: 25.6% published vs 37.4% segment mean) — enough to
   trigger a wrong "fix."

## 7. Replication notes and ambiguities

- **Learned set.** Section 16.5 unions `_skills` + species learnings + class
  learnings. Verified in data: species learnings and class learnings are
  identical for all 504 species, and a fresh actor's `_skills` (built by
  `setGanjukoSpecies` learning the class list, RMMZ-sorted, trimmed to the
  last 4 by `enforceFourMoveLimit`) is a subset of that learnset — so the
  union is exactly the Lv≤50 learnset, which is what the sim uses. An
  evolved actor in-game could carry up to 3 stale ids from its previous
  species' learnset in `_skills`; immaterial at Lv50 because the four fixed
  slot picks fill the pool before `learnedBest`/backfill are consulted.
  Literal consequence, flagged as designed behaviour rather than ambiguity:
  **at Lv50, `learnedBest` and the backfill branch are dead code** — pools
  cannot reflect species identity at this level.
- **`_s16_skillStrength`** was replicated exactly (damage.type 3 → flat 15;
  otherwise formula evaluated against the battler itself at full HP, × 1.5
  if STAB). At Lv50 it only feeds the dead branches above, so its
  self-vs-self convention cannot affect mode C results.
- **Enemies are modelled as actors.** In the real engine, enemies do not
  fight with the best-4: 16.5 stores the *full* pool candidate list on
  enemies and Section 16.4's AI substitutes the best-scoring action with
  probability 40% (wild) / 80% (trainer) / 95% (gym/Stadium). Per the task
  definition, mode C fields both sides with the best-4 under greedy
  selection. Expected direction of the difference: real wild battles are
  somewhat more forgiving than mode C; trainer/gym battles are close to it.
- **Sim abstractions inherited from v1** (unchanged, so modes stay
  comparable): status moves are a flat 30-point utility with no state, heal
  scores can exceed what the real engine's AI would choose, MP is ignored,
  and damaging moves' rider effects (poison etc.) are not modelled. These
  flatter some types' utility slots (Fire's 80% heal) and flatten others'
  (Bulk Up / Obsidian Bastion read as Brace-equivalents).
